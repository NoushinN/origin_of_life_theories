From Chemistry to Life: Scientific Theories on the Origin of Life

Chapter 2 Primordial Soup and Chemical Evolution

2.1 Core Idea

The primordial soup model proposes that organic building blocks accumulated through abiotic chemistry in early Earth environments.

2.2 Historical Context

The classical form of this idea was articulated by Oparin and Haldane in the early twentieth century and later gained empirical support through Miller’s spark-discharge experiment (Miller 1953).

2.3 Mechanistic Basis

The model proposes that atmospheric gases, water, and energy sources such as lightning or ultraviolet radiation generated amino acids, nucleobases, and other organics.

2.4 What the Theory Explains Well

This theory is strongest at explaining the origin of prebiotic chemical building blocks.

2.5 What Makes It Plausible

Laboratory chemistry and meteoritic organics both support the plausibility of abiotic organic synthesis.

2.6 Key Experimental / Observational Support

The Miller-Urey framework and carbonaceous meteorites strongly support abiotic precursor synthesis (Miller 1953).

2.7 Major Gaps and Critiques

Its major weakness is organizational: it explains ingredients but not heredity, structured metabolism, or evolutionary persistence.

2.8 Current Scientific Standing

Primordial soup remains foundational but incomplete. It is widely accepted as a strong model for precursor synthesis, but not as a complete model of abiogenesis.

Dimension Assessment
Strength Strong for building blocks
Plausibility High
Main Gap Organization and heredity
Current Standing Foundational but incomplete
