From Chemistry to Life: Scientific Theories on the Origin of Life

Chapter 5 Lipid World and Protocell-First Models

5.1 Core idea

The lipid world hypothesis proposes that life-like systems began when amphiphilic molecules self-assembled into membrane-bound vesicles. These vesicles created compartments that concentrated molecules, protected fragile chemistry, and allowed protocell-like systems to form.

5.2 Scientific mechanism

A possible sequence is:

  1. Fatty acids or other amphiphilic molecules formed abiotically.
  2. These molecules self-assembled into vesicles in water.
  3. Vesicles encapsulated RNA, peptides, or metabolic molecules.
  4. Vesicles grew, divided, and created selection among protocell populations.
  5. More stable membrane-genetic-metabolic systems emerged.

5.3 Questions this theory can answer

Lipid and protocell-first models answer:

  • How did compartments arise before modern cells?
  • How could molecules become concentrated and protected?
  • How could early chemical systems become individuals subject to selection?

5.4 Explanatory strength

The major strength is compartmentalization. Without compartments, useful molecules diffuse away. Membranes allow chemical systems to maintain internal conditions and link molecular function to a specific protocell.

5.5 Gaps and limitations

Membranes alone are not alive. Lipid-world theories must still explain:

  • heredity;
  • metabolism;
  • controlled growth and division;
  • how membrane systems became coupled to RNA or metabolic networks.

5.6 Research opportunities

Future work can explore protocell growth, vesicle division, encapsulation of RNA, membrane permeability, and how simple lipid systems interact with minerals and wet-dry cycles.
